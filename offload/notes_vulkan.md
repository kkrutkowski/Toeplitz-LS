The real danger zone is what happens next with GLSL/Vulkan. If SYCL—with C++ templates, `if constexpr`, and re-includable macro engines doing the heavy lifting—still pushed the C++ side past 11.5k lines, hand-writing the Vulkan compute equivalent will easily blow past 40k LOC. Two things are worth deciding before you write line one of GLSL:

* **Don't hand-write the shaders—generate them.** Without templates, manually maintaining unrolled butterflies, twiddle ladders, XOR-swizzled transposes, and triple-precision variants in raw GLSL is pure duplication hell. VkFFT dodged maintaining 100k+ lines of hand-written shaders by keeping a ~3k-line generator that emits unrolled, constant-inlined shaders for each `(logN, precision, subgroup_size, smem)` configuration. Since you already proved the exact DIF schedules in SYCL, a small generator script emitting GLSL/SPIR-V keeps the source footprint tiny and stops the SYCL and Vulkan math from drifting apart.
* **Lock down Dekker's error terms in SPIR-V from day one.** In GLSL you'll run straight into the Vulkan twin of the `ptxas --fmad` fight: you need the `precise` qualifier on your `TwoSum` and `TwoProd` intermediates so the shader compiler preserves operation order and emits SPIR-V `NoContraction` instead of letting vendor drivers silently contract away your low-order error terms.
